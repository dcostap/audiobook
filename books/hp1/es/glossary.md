# Voice

- The narrator is dryly amused by the Dursleys' self-satisfied “normality”; keep the irony understated.
- Events are often filtered through Vernon's stubbornly ordinary point of view, which makes the strange details funnier.
- Balance brisk comic details with moments of unease and, at the end, a warm, fairy-tale cadence.
- Keep the occasional narrator aside conversational; don't over-explain the magic.

# Characters' speech

- **tú or usted:** Dumbledore and McGonagall use *usted* with each other. Hagrid uses *usted* with both of them. They use *tú* with Hagrid.

- Mr Dursley: gruff and self-important; he dismisses anything outside his idea of normal.
- Mrs Dursley (Petunia): clipped, sharp and socially disapproving.
- Dumbledore: measured, courteous and calm, with gentle, understated wit.
- Professor McGonagall: precise and formal, but brisk and impatient when worried.
- Hagrid: broad, informal rural dialect with dropped sounds (“an’”, “ter”); suggest it lightly rather than using heavy phonetic spelling.
- The unnamed old man: high, breathless cheer, with ceremonious and exuberant phrasing.
- Dudley: childish and demanding; make “Shan’t!” a blunt, emphatic refusal.
- Ted and Jim McGuffin: polished broadcast voices; Jim adds a playful aside.
