# Task: write the Spanish glossary of a book

You prepare the glossary that the translators of this book use. The translators are other agents. Each one translates a small part of a chapter, so the glossary keeps them consistent.

## Read

1. `guides/translation-es.md`: the style guide. The glossary must fit it.
2. The chapters given in your task (`books/<id>/<nn>/text.md`). Read all of them.

## Write

Write `books/<id>/es/glossary.md`. If it already exists, keep its entries and add the new ones. Change an entry only if it is wrong.

It has these sections:

1. **Names and terms.** One table: English | Spanish | Hint. Include:
   - people, places, schools, shops, companies, invented words, and titles of things
   - forms of address (Mr, Mrs, Professor) and how to say them in Spanish
   - words or phrases that come back and need the same gloss each time

   For "Spanish", use the form of the published Spanish edition (Salamandra, translated in Spain), if you are sure of it. If you are not sure, give your best Spanish form and add "(?)". Keep a name in English when the Spanish edition keeps it.

   "Hint" is optional. It is a short instruction for the translators. Example: "*Muggle*: keep it; the note explains it is an invented word, at its first use."
2. **Voice.** 3 to 6 short points about the narrator's voice and tone, and how the Spanish translation can keep it. Example: dry irony about the Dursleys.
3. **Characters' speech.** One line for each character who speaks in a special way (register, dialect, habits).

## Rules

- **No spoilers.** Use only what the given chapters tell. Do not write what you know about later chapters or later books, because the translators could repeat it in a note.
- Keep it short: at most about 150 lines. Only include what helps consistency.
- Write the glossary in English, with Spanish forms in the Spanish column.

## Reply

When you finish, reply with the number of entries and a list of the forms you marked "(?)".
